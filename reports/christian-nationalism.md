# Christian nationalism in the PCA: research pass

Written 2026-11-20. All new events have `status: draft` and `visibility: public`. `uv run -q tools/build.py --strict` passes with exit code 0, and the build prints no warnings or errors. No event in this pass rests only on a social media post.

## Scope

This pass covers how the PCA has dealt with Christian nationalism and the questions close to it:

- **The civil magistrate.** WCF 23 in the 1646 text and in the American revision of 1788, which the PCA adopted in 1973.
- **The spirituality of the church.** The church's "declarative and ministerial" task (WCF 31.4, BCO 3), and the times when the Assembly addressed civil rulers.
- **Theonomy and Christian reconstruction.** WCF 19.4 and the judicial law, from 1978 to 1996.
- **Ethnic nationalism and kinism.** Only where it is tied to the debate (2018, 2025, 2026).
- **The recent debate.** Overtures (2024–2025), the GA52 study committee, the 2026 partial report, and the first published responses.

Events outside the PCA are limited to Stephen Wolfe's 2022 book and the first PCA review of it. The ARP and RPCNA appear only as the bodies that GA52 joined.

## Record, position, study and opinion

**Record (what bodies did).** The adoption of the Standards (1973), the theonomy referrals and cases (1978–1996), the 1984 and 1988 church/state actions, the GA52 votes and the GA53 action all come from the GA minutes, overtures or news reports. The GA53 action rests on news reports (Christian Post, byFaith) and on the Stated Clerk's summary, because the M53GA minutes are not yet a source.

**Position (what the Assembly adopted).** These are the 1973 Message, the 1979 theonomy statement, the 1983 answers to Gulf Coast, the 1984 policy on obeying the law, the 1988 summary positions, and the 2025 condemnation of race-superiority teaching. The 2018 race report was only "received" and distributed; the Assembly did not adopt its text as a statement.

**Study (not adopted).** The 2026 partial report is not an adopted position. GA53 only made it available and extended the committee. The report itself says that only church courts can declare a view constitutional or unconstitutional. The events say "the committee advised."

**Opinion.** Wolfe (via his publisher's page), DeYoung, Ball (twice), Martin, Tooley and Smith. The event text names each author.

### Points where the record and later accounts differ

These are noted, not resolved:

1. **The 1979 wording.** The 11th GA (M11GA p. 96) said that M7GA p. 115 was "incorrect" and left out the qualifier "in so far as this view is in accord with paragraph a above." The printed page 115 does not have the qualifier. The 2026 partial report (p. 2724) introduces item 22 as what the minutes "have … on page 115," and its quotation includes the qualifier. The event `ga7-theonomy-statement` says "as printed in its minutes," and `ga11-advice-on-theonomy` records the correction.
2. **The 1983 Deuteronomy 13 answer.** The partial report summarizes the 11th GA as stating that the penal sanctions of Deuteronomy 13 applied to Israel's theocratic era. The record also has 29 recorded negative votes and a separate adopted proviso: "this declaration shall not be used by the courts of the Church to bind the conscience of elders in the PCA" (M11GA p. 97).
3. **"The PCA has spoken."** In 1992 the SJC wrote that "prior General Assemblies have not spoken clearly on theonomy" (M20GA p. 199). In 1996 the 24th GA declined a study because the Assembly "has previously studied this matter" (M24GA p. 280). In 2026 the partial report wrote that the PCA drew a line "over forty years ago" (p. 2725).
4. **Overture 48 at GA52 and Overture 13 at GA49.** The extracted text of M52GA p. 115 still contains the struck words about a pastoral-letter committee. The page image (PDF p. 117) shows that the Overtures Committee struck that request with a double line and put the race-superiority condemnation in its place. The event follows the page image. Two other pages agree with it: M52GA p. 15 lists only two ad interim committees, and the Overture 49 grounds speak of "two study committees." Christianity Daily and some other news reports do not mention this. The same problem occurs at GA49: the extracted text of M49GA pp. 98–99 contains Ascension Presbytery's "humble petition" and its "cases extraordinary" paragraph, but the page images (PDF pp. 101–102) show them struck. The Assembly adopted the 1978 statement instead. The event follows the page image. Both readings rest on the page images, because no quote can show a strike-through. I checked the GA51 amendment (M51GA PDF pp. 92–93) in the same way; only its last paragraph is struck, and the event does not use that paragraph.
5. **The "unamended" catechisms (1973).** M1GA p. 31 adopts "the unamended Westminster Larger and Shorter Catechisms." I did not find what "unamended" meant here. It may refer to 20th-century PCUS amendments, not to the 1788 revision. The partial report (p. 2715) treats WLC 109 as revised in 1788. This needs a source.
