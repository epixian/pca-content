---
title: New Perspective on Paul
---

**The New Perspective on Paul** is an academic movement in New Testament studies, associated since the 1960s with Krister Stendahl, E. P. Sanders, James D. G. Dunn and N. T. Wright. It rereads Paul's teaching on the law and justification in the light of first-century Judaism. Its scholars hold a variety of views; The committee noted that N. T. Wright "has had a significant influence in recent years on the broader evangelical and Reformed communities."

In the PCA it came up together with the [Federal Vision](/issues/federal-vision/): presbytery reports from 2005 to 2007 and the Assembly's study committee of 2006–2007 examined both. The committee studied the New Perspective mainly through Wright's writings and kept it apart from the Federal Vision, because of "important differences between them, not the least being their views on Scripture and the Reformed Confessions." The 35th General Assembly (2007) recommended the committee's nine declarations, made "in light of the controversy surrounding the NPP and FV", as "a faithful exposition of the Westminster Standards".
