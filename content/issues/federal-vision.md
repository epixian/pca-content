---
title: Federal Vision and the New Perspective on Paul
---

Two sets of teaching, which the PCA's study committee of 2006–2007 examined together.

**The Federal Vision**, also called Auburn Avenue Theology, is a set of views on the covenant, baptism, election and justification. The name "Auburn Avenue" comes from Auburn Avenue Presbyterian Church in Monroe, Louisiana, a PCA church that hosted pastors' conferences where these views were taught, among them one in 2002. Its proponents preferred "Federal Vision", the title of their 2004 book. They did not agree on every point, and some denied that there was a movement at all. The views that the study committee found contrary to the Westminster Standards include these: that a person is "elect" by membership in the visible church, but can lose that election; that water baptism unites each baptized person to Christ with his saving benefits; that a person can be united to Christ and not persevere; and the denial that Christ's obedience is imputed to believers.

**The New Perspective on Paul** is an academic movement in New Testament studies, associated with Krister Stendahl, E. P. Sanders, James D. G. Dunn and N. T. Wright. Since the 1960s it has reread Paul's teaching on the law and justification in the light of first-century Judaism. The committee studied it mainly through Wright's writings, and treated it apart from the Federal Vision.

In 2007 the 35th General Assembly recommended the committee's nine declarations "as a faithful exposition of the Westminster Standards". The church-court cases that followed concern ministers in four presbyteries: Louisiana (TE Steve Wilkins), Pacific Northwest (TE Peter Leithart), Siouxlands (TEs Greg Lawrence and Joshua Moon) and Missouri (TE Jeffrey Meyers).
