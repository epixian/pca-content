**The Federal Vision**, also called Auburn Avenue Theology, is a set of views on the covenant, baptism, election and justification, taught by some Reformed ministers from the early 2000s. The name "Auburn Avenue" comes from Auburn Avenue Presbyterian Church in Monroe, Louisiana, a PCA church that hosted pastors' conferences where these views were taught, among them one in 2002. Its proponents preferred "Federal Vision", the title of their 2004 book. They did not agree on every point, and some denied that there was a movement at all.

The views that the Assembly's study committee found contrary to the Westminster Standards include these: that a person is "elect" by membership in the visible church, but can lose that election; that water baptism unites each baptized person to Christ with his saving benefits; that a person can be united to Christ and not persevere; and the denial that Christ's obedience is imputed to believers.

The committee studied the Federal Vision together with the [New Perspective on Paul](/issues/new-perspective-on-paul/), but kept the two apart: "while there may be some similarities as to their views, there are important differences between them, not the least being their views on Scripture and the Reformed Confessions." In 2007 the 35th General Assembly recommended the committee's nine declarations "as a faithful exposition of the Westminster Standards". The church-court cases that followed concern ministers in four presbyteries: Louisiana (TE Steve Wilkins), Pacific Northwest (TE Peter Leithart), Siouxlands (TEs Greg Lawrence and Joshua Moon) and Missouri (TE Jeffrey Meyers).
