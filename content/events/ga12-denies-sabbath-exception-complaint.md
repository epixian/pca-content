A ruling elder complained that Central Carolina Presbytery had ordained a licentiate who said that the fourth commandment did not have to be observed because the New Testament did not cover it. The judicial commission of the 12th General Assembly found that the only substantive point was whether the presbytery's allowed Sabbath exception was constitutional. It did not sustain the complaint and confirmed the presbytery's decision. Its opinion said that the commission disagreed on what the position on the Sabbath and subscription ought to be, and that the complaint's specifications did not support the complaint. The report was adopted, and several commissioners recorded negative votes.
